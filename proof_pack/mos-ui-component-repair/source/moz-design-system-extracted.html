Typography

Libre Baskerville handles every heading — generous, editorial, with italics for emphasis. DM Sans handles everything else: body copy, UI labels, overlines, even token names. Two fonts, no more.

Display The quiet hum of growth. 92 / 1.0 · −0.045em
H1 Marketing that listens first. 56 / 1.08 · −0.02em
H2 Studies your market. 44 / 1.1 · −0.018em
H3 Understands the customer 26 / 1.2 · 700
Lede Moz reads your codebase, support tickets, and product launches — and turns them into campaigns that ship themselves. 21 / 1.45 · DM Sans 400
Body Standard body copy is set in DM Sans at 16 pixels with a 1.55 leading. It reads quietly underneath the serif and supports long-form sections without fatigue. Use it for paragraphs, descriptions, and most UI labels. 16 / 1.55 · DM Sans 400
Small Captions, meta, and dense UI rows live at the small size. 13 / 1.55 · DM Sans 400

Space, shape & depth

A 4-pixel grid governs all spacing. Radii are rounded but never balloon. Shadows are quiet — soft greys, never blue glows except for buttons.

02 / Brand

The mark, the wordmark, the lockup.

A bold filled source projects two echo dots — the signal weakens as it travels. It says what Moz does without spelling it out: amplify the message, reach further, fade gracefully.

Mark exploration — megaphone & stamp variants

Four megaphone takes (A1–A4) and four stamp takes (B1–B4). Each variant turns one knob: cone angle, output gesture, aperture shape, weight. Pick the row, then the variant.

Moz

A small kit, used heavily.

Buttons are pill-shaped, bold, and flat. Primary stays black; hover darkens with a subtle top-edge sheen — no shadow, no color flip, just the kind of surface response you'd expect from Linear or Vercel.

Choice cards

For onboarding and survey moments — large, tappable, scannable. A stack of full-width cards beats a radio group every time. Each card is icon + title + subtitle + selection ring. Works as single-select (radio) or multi-select (checkbox).
